# 11 Meeting Templates

## Meeting Template 1: Weekly Team Meeting

| Section | What to include |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Align priorities, blockers, decisions, and ownership for the week. |
| Pre-read | Last week's action list, current project status, open risks. |
| Agenda | 1. Top 3 priorities. 2. Progress since last week. 3. Blockers. 4. Decisions needed. 5. Action items. |
| Required output | Decisions, action items, owners, deadlines, follow-up date. |
| AI instruction | Turn the notes into a clean operating summary and flag unclear ownership. |

## Meeting Template 2: One-on-One Meeting

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Check-in | What feels heavy right now? What is going well? |
| Work | What is your most important work this week? Where are you stuck? |
| Feedback | What feedback do you need from me? What feedback do I need to share? |
| Growth | What skill or responsibility should we build next? |
| Follow-up | What will you do? What will I do? When do we check back? |

## Meeting Template 3: Project Kickoff Meeting

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Outcome | What are we trying to deliver? |
| Scope | What is included? What is out of scope? |
| Roles | Who owns what? Who approves? Who must be consulted or informed? |
| Timeline | What are the key milestones? |
| Risks | What could slow us down? What is the backup plan? |
| Output | Project brief, owner list, milestone plan, risk plan. |

## Meeting Template 4: Decision Meeting

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Decision | What decision must be made today? |
| Options | What are the real options? |
| Evidence | What facts support each option? |
| Trade-offs | What do we gain and give up? |
| Risks | What could go wrong? How reversible is the choice? |
| Output | Decision, rationale, owner, deadline, communication plan. |

## Meeting Template 5: Problem-Solving Meeting

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Problem | What is happening? What is the impact? |
| Symptoms | What are we seeing? What data supports it? |
| Causes | What might be causing this? What do we need to verify? |
| Fixes | What are short-term and long-term fixes? |
| Output | Root cause hypothesis, action plan, verification owner. |

## Meeting Template 6: Performance Feedback Meeting

| Section | What to cover |
|---|---|
| Opening | State the purpose calmly and directly. |
| Facts | Share specific examples and dates when possible. |
| Impact | Explain the effect on work, customers, team, or timeline. |
| Employee view | Ask what they saw and what support they need. |
| Expectation | Define what needs to change. |
| Follow-up | Set check-in date and success evidence. |

## Meeting Template 7: Retrospective Meeting

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Wins | What worked and should be repeated? |
| Misses | What slowed us down or hurt quality? |
| Causes | Why did those issues happen? |
| Learning | What should we change next time? |
| Output | Keep / stop / start list and owner for each improvement. |

## Meeting Template 8: Change Communication Meeting

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Why | Why is this change happening now? |
| What | What changes for the team? |
| Impact | Who is affected and how? |
| Support | What help, training, or transition time is available? |
| Questions | What concerns must be heard and answered? |
| Output | FAQ, transition plan, support owners. |

## Meeting Template 9: Workload Review Meeting

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Capacity | Who is overloaded? Who has room? |
| Deadlines | Which deadlines are at risk? |
| Dependencies | Who is waiting on whom? |
| Rebalance | What should move, pause, or get help? |
| Output | Updated owner list, workload changes, escalations. |

## Meeting Template 10: Employee Development Meeting

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| Direction | What future role or responsibility is the employee interested in? |
| Current strengths | What can they already do well? |
| Gaps | What skills, exposure, or judgment still need to grow? |
| Practice | What stretch assignment can build the gap? |
| Support | What coaching, training, or mentoring is needed? |
| Output | Growth plan with 30/60/90-day actions. |

## Meeting Template 11: Your 1:1 With Your Manager (Managing Up)

Use this when you are the one being managed. A 1:1 is not a status report. Your manager already saw your weekly update. This is your time, a rare stretch where they are fully listening, so use it to show the value you are creating and where you want to grow. Four simple moves:

| Move | The common way (skip this) | A better way to say it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Open with your impact | "This week I worked on..." and then a list of tasks | "The biggest difference I made this week was [result]. It helped [who or which goal], and now [what changed]." |
| 2. Ask for one specific piece of feedback | "Any feedback for me?" | "On [the thing you did this week], I tried [a new approach]. Did that work well, or would you do it a bit differently next time?" |
| 3. Turn a need into a team win | Staying quiet, or "I'd love to help if there's ever a chance" | "I noticed [project or team] could use a hand with [X]. I've done [something similar] before, so I think I could pitch in. Could I join [the next step]?" |
| 4. Close on the next checkpoint | "Okay, that's everything. Thanks." | "So to recap: I'll focus on [X] this month and update you on [Y] next time. I'm working toward [goal], and I'd love to check in on [growth or a promotion] when the time feels right." |

Why these four moves work:

- Opening with your impact instead of your task list sets the tone for the whole conversation. You come across as someone creating value, not someone whose work needs tracking.
- A specific feedback question is easy for your manager to answer well, and it shows you are deliberately working on a skill.
- Framing a need as a team benefit makes it easy to say yes. You are offering a solution, not asking for a favor.
- Ending on a checkpoint keeps your growth on their radar and ties today's talk to where you are headed.

A simple agenda to bring, instead of a list of task updates:

1. The biggest result you created this week.
2. One skill you are practicing, and the feedback you want on it.
3. One thing you need from your manager so you can do more.
4. Your next growth goal.

Keep it honest. Lead with real results and real numbers. Pick the angle your manager cares about, like whether the project stays on track, and keep the facts exactly as they happened. If it was a quiet week, share what you learned or unblocked instead. Never invent a result.

For managers: you can share this template with someone on your team who freezes up in 1:1s or gives you a flat status report. It hands them a simple, confident way to use the time well, and it makes the conversation more useful for both of you.
